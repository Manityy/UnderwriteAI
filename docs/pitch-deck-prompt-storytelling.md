## PROMPT START

Create a **simple, story-driven, visually rich 10-slide pitch deck** in **English** for a hackathon called **"Automate or die"**.

**Team:** Pivot — Elaa, Imen, Aziz

**Product:** UnderwriteAI — *underwriting intelligence for AI risk.*

**Golden rule for this deck:** This is a STORY, not a document. Very little text per slide. Big visuals, big numbers, short sentences. A non-technical person (and a busy insurance executive) must understand it instantly. Never more than ~20 words of body text per slide. Use one big idea per slide.

**Tone:** Human, clear, confident. Like telling a story to a friend. Avoid jargon and hype. When you use an insurance term, keep it plain.

**Visual style:** Clean, modern insurtech look. Dark navy background, white text, one bright accent color (teal or amber). Large numbers as hero elements. Simple icons and simple diagrams. No dense paragraphs. No cheesy robot stock photos.

Follow this exact slide-by-slide story:

---

### SLIDE 1 — Hook (Title)
- Huge title: **UnderwriteAI**
- One line under it: *We help insurers cover the risks of AI.*
- Small: Team Pivot — Elaa, Imen, Aziz · "Automate or die" hackathon
- Visual: a simple shield over an AI spark / neural icon
- Tiny teaser line at the bottom: *"Every company is racing to use AI. Almost no one is insuring what could go wrong."*

---

### SLIDE 2 — The shock (set the stage)
Big-number slide. Show three bold statistics as the hero, minimal words:

- **88%** — of organizations now use AI in at least one part of their business (up from 78% a year ago)
- **79%** — already use generative AI
- **+56%** — jump in reported AI incidents in a single year (233 in 2024, a record)

One short caption under them: *AI is everywhere. So are its mistakes.*

Sources in small print: McKinsey State of AI 2025; Stanford AI Index 2025.

---

### SLIDE 3 — The story (make it real)
Headline: **Meet "NovaBank."**

Tell a tiny 3-step visual story (icons + one line each, like a comic strip):
1. NovaBank launches an AI chatbot to advise customers. Everyone's happy.
2. The chatbot confidently gives a customer **wrong financial advice** — and leaks another customer's personal data.
3. The customer sues. Now the big question appears on screen: **"Who pays?"**

Keep text tiny. The punchline "Who pays?" should be large.

---

### SLIDE 4 — The gap (the problem, simply)
Headline: **Insurers want to cover this. But they can't measure it.**

Three short points with icons:
- AI is a **black box** — clients won't share their models or code
- Insurers have **no standard way** to judge how risky an AI system is
- So today they either **say no**, or **guess the price**

One line at the bottom: *You can't price what you can't see.*

---

### SLIDE 5 — The solution (one clear sentence)
Headline: **UnderwriteAI is an X-ray for AI risk.**

- One simple sentence: *We give insurers a clear risk score for their clients' AI — without ever needing the secret code.*
- Important clarifying line: **We don't sell insurance. We help insurers sell it smarter.**
- Visual: input (a company's AI) → UnderwriteAI → output (a clean risk score: Low / Medium / High)

---

### SLIDE 6 — How we calculate the risk (THE key slide — keep it simple!)
Headline: **How we measure the risk — in 3 steps**

Show a simple left-to-right flow with 3 friendly boxes and an arrow to a final score. NO technical jargon.

**1. Ask (the questionnaire)**
*The company tells us how they use their AI: what it does, what data it touches, is a human checking it.*

**2. Test (our two AI agents)**
*Agent 1 acts like a "troublemaker" and tries to break the AI with tricky questions. Agent 2 acts like a "judge" and grades how the AI responded — did it lie, leak data, or give unsafe answers?*

**3. Score**
*We combine the answers + the test results into one clear risk score: Low, Medium, or High — with the reasons and fixes.*

Bottom caption (small, honest): *More evidence, less guessing. We reduce uncertainty — the insurer still sets the final price.*

Visual idea: box "Ask" + box "Test (Troublemaker AI -> Judge AI)" + box "Score" -> a gauge showing Low/Medium/High.

---

### SLIDE 7 — See it in action (proof)
Headline: **Back to NovaBank.**

Show a simple "report card" visual:
- NovaBank said: *"A human always checks. Data is safe."*
- Our test found: *Chatbot leaked sample data 2 times out of 50, and gave confident wrong advice.*
- UnderwriteAI score: **HIGH RISK** (big, in the accent color)
- What the insurer does: *charge more, or ask NovaBank to fix it first.*

Keep it looking like a clean scorecard, not paragraphs.

---

### SLIDE 8 — The opportunity (why this is big + why now)
Big-number slide again, minimal text:

- **$0.8B → $22B+** — the AI insurance market is projected to grow massively by 2034 (one of the fastest-growing insurance lines)
- **$166B** — projected size of the MENA AI market by 2030 (growing ~45% a year)
- One line: *A brand-new type of insurance is being born. We build the tool that makes it possible.*

Small print: market forecasts from Grand View Research, and specialty-insurance market reports (see notes).

---

### SLIDE 9 — The business (how we make money) — simple BMC
Headline: **Simple business model**

Show as 4 clean icon-cards, not a full canvas grid:
- **Who buys:** Insurance companies (and reinsurers)
- **What they get:** A fast, standard AI risk score for every client
- **How we charge:** Pay per assessment + yearly platform subscription
- **Why they stay:** We re-check AI risks every renewal

One line: *We sit inside the insurer's process — they keep the client, we power the decision.*

---

### SLIDE 10 — The ask (close strong)
Headline: **Help us make AI insurable.**

- Team: **Pivot** — Elaa · Imen · Aziz
- Three tiny asks with icons:
  1. **Win** Automate or die
  2. **Pilot** with a real insurer
  3. **Guidance** from insurance experts in the room
- Big closing line: *"Insurers already price the risk of fires, floods, and cyberattacks. We help them price the risk of AI."*

---

**Design reminders for the whole deck:**
- Max ~20 words of body text per slide. Let visuals and big numbers do the talking.
- One idea per slide. If a slide feels crowded, cut words.
- Reuse the "NovaBank" story on slides 3 and 7 so it feels connected.
- Add short speaker notes (1–2 sentences) per slide for a 5-minute pitch.
